Friday/Sunday Wedding Hack: Vendor Pricing Decoded

By Olivia Bennett June 12, 2026 5 min read

Friday/Sunday Wedding Hack: Vendor Pricing Decoded

Priya sat in my office with two venue quotes on her lap. Same space. Same month. Same number of guests. One said Saturday: $8,500. The other said Friday: $4,200. She looked at the Saturday quote, then at the Friday quote, then back at the Saturday quote. Her finger tapped the paper twice.

"Why is Saturday $4,000 more?" she asked.

"Because it is Saturday," I said. "That is the only reason."

She booked the Friday. Saved $4,300. Used the money to add a photo booth and a late-night churro cart. Her guests still talk about those churros.

Friday weddings average 15-30% cheaper than Saturday weddings. Same venue. Same food. Same photographer. Just a different day on the calendar.

When I was planning my wedding, I assumed Saturday was the only option. Every venue website showed Saturday pricing first. Every vendor asked "what is your date?" with a tone that implied Saturday was the default. Then I asked about Friday. The venue coordinator sighed. "We can do Friday. It is $3,000 less." Just like that. $3,000 for changing one word.

I asked about Sunday. She sighed again. "Sunday is $4,000 less, but you have to end by 9 PM." I took Friday. Saved $3,000. Spent that money on better food.

Select Saturday. See the price. Select Friday. Watch the number drop. That is not magic. That is supply and demand. Saturdays sell out first, so venues charge more. Fridays and Sundays have less demand, so they discount.

Priya saved $7,000 by moving her wedding from Saturday to Friday. She had 120 guests. The venue was $6,000 cheaper. The photographer knocked off $800. The DJ took $200 off. The florist offered free delivery on Friday but charged $150 on Saturday. She used the savings to add a photo booth and a late-night churro cart.

I asked her if any guests complained about the Friday date. She said "three people could not come. One was a cousin I barely know. The other two were coworkers who RSVP'd yes to the Saturday but then changed their plans to make Friday work." She did not lose anyone who mattered.

Here is the truth about Friday weddings. Some guests will not come. That is okay. Your wedding is not a summons. The people who love you will take a day off. The people who do not — you do not need them there anyway.

But Friday is not the only option. Let me decode the full pricing calendar.

Saturday evening (peak, 5-11 PM). Most expensive. Highest demand. Venue rental $8,000-15,000. Catering minimums $10,000-20,000. Vendors charge full price. This is for couples who have unlimited budgets or family help.

Friday evening (5-11 PM). Second most expensive. Venue rental $5,000-10,000. Catering minimums $7,000-15,000. Vendors may discount 10-20%. Best trade-off between savings and convenience.

Sunday evening (3-9 PM). Cheaper than Friday. Venue rental $4,000-8,000. Catering minimums $6,000-12,000. But you have to end earlier. Some guests will not stay late because they work Monday. Still a solid option.

Sunday brunch (10 AM-2 PM). Much cheaper. Venue rental $2,000-5,000. Catering is breakfast or lunch — $20-40 per person instead of $50-100. Mimosas instead of open bar. Done by 2 PM. I have planned Sunday brunch weddings for under $10,000 total. Guests love them.

Thursday or Monday. The cheapest. Venue rental $1,500-4,000. Vendors will give their biggest discounts because these days are hard to fill. But many guests can not take two days off. Only works if your guest list is local or retired.

Weekday (Tuesday/Wednesday). Almost free compared to Saturday. But do not do it unless you are eloping. Most people can not attend.

Select Saturday. It says $150 per person. Select Sunday. It says $110 per person. Multiply by 100 guests — that is $4,000 saved. Right there. Without changing anything else.

Vendor availability is the hidden benefit of non-Saturday weddings. The best vendors book Saturdays a year in advance. If you want the popular photographer, the DJ everyone recommends, the florist with the great Instagram — they are free on Fridays. You will get your first choice, not your third.

Lauren wanted a specific photographer. The photographer's Saturday rate was $6,000. Lauren's budget for photography was $3,500. She asked about Friday. The photographer said $4,000. She asked about Thursday. The photographer said $2,800. She moved her wedding to Thursday. She saved $3,200 on photography alone. Her guest list dropped by 20 people — mostly distant relatives she did not care about. She did not regret it.

She started with a Saturday budget of $30k. Then she switched to Thursday. The calculator automatically lowered the estimated costs for venue, catering, photography, and DJ. Her new total was $22k. She added $3k to her honeymoon and saved $5k. Thirty-three percent less for the same quality.

Saturday is not never worth it. Some couples have family flying in from overseas. Some have traditions that require Saturday. Some just want a Saturday. That is fine. But at least know what you are paying for. Saturday is a luxury. Treat it like one.

If you are on the fence, try this test. Call three vendors. Ask for their Saturday price and their Friday price. Write down the difference. Add it up. Then ask yourself: "What else could I do with that money?" A better honeymoon? A down payment? A year of date nights? That is your answer.

Lauren — the Thursday bride — she spent the $8,000 she saved on a two-week trip to Japan. She told me "I will never look back and wish I had a Saturday wedding. I will look back at photos of us eating ramen in Tokyo."
