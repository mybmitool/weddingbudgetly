How We Saved $8,000 by Getting Married on a Friday

By Olivia Bennett June 02, 2026 6 min read

How We Saved $8,000 by Getting Married on a Friday

The Saturday wedding premium is real. And it is robbery.

When Daniel and I first started planning, every venue we looked at had two price sheets. One for Saturday. One for every other day of the week. At a vineyard in the Willamette Valley, Saturday cost $9,000 for the space alone. Friday was $5,000. Sunday was $4,000. I asked the coordinator why. She said "Saturday is our highest demand day." I asked what was different about the space on Friday versus Saturday. She said "nothing. Same space. Same staff. Same setup."

Nothing. Same everything. Four thousand dollars more for the privilege of a calendar square.

I walked away from that tour with my notebook open, doing the math. If we moved to Friday, we could save $4,000 just on venue rental. But that was not all. I started calling vendors. Same question: "What is your Saturday price vs. your Friday price?"

Photographer: Saturday $4,500, Friday $3,200. Savings $1,300.
DJ: Saturday $1,800, Friday $1,200. Savings $600.
Catering minimums: Saturday $8,000, Friday $5,000. Savings $3,000 — but we were doing food trucks anyway, so that one did not apply to us.
Florist delivery fee: Saturday $250, Friday $150. Savings $100.

Add it up. Venue $4,000. Photography $1,300. DJ $600. Florist $100. That is $6,000 before we even started. We ended up saving about $8,000 total by choosing a Friday.

That is not nothing. That is a honeymoon.

We plugged in 85 guests, Portland, "casual dinner." It spit out $95 per person. Multiply by 85, that is $8,075 for food, drinks, and basic rentals. Then we started playing with date options. The tool had a toggle: "Saturday premium" vs. "Weekday discount." Flipping that toggle changed the per-person estimate from $95 to $72. That is $23 per person times 85 people — almost $2,000. Plus the fixed venue and vendor savings. The numbers were undeniable.

But Friday comes with trade-offs. I am not going to pretend there are not any.

About 15 of our guests could not come because of work. A cousin who is a teacher said "I can not take a Friday off in May — it is testing season." A friend who works retail said her manager denied her request because two other people were already off that day. We knew this going in. We weighed the savings against the missing faces. For us, it was worth it. For some couples, it might not be.

Another trade-off: Friday wedding means Friday rehearsal and Thursday setup. That is an extra day of coordination. I had to take Thursday off work. Daniel took Thursday off too. Our wedding party had to be there by 2 PM on Friday. Some of them took half-days. It is more disruptive. There is no way around that.

Also, Friday night traffic in Portland is brutal. Several guests got stuck on I-5 and arrived late. We held the ceremony for twenty extra minutes. People were frazzled. If you are considering a Friday wedding, add a note on your invitation: "Leave early. Traffic is real."

We created two scenarios. Scenario A: Saturday wedding with all the premium pricing. Total came to $23,000. Scenario B: Friday wedding with adjusted vendor quotes. Total came to $15,000. That is an $8,000 difference — exactly what we saved. Seeing those two numbers side by side made the decision feel less like a sacrifice and more like a smart trade.

Maya, a coworker from my planning days, was dead set on a Saturday. She said "I do not want to inconvenience my guests." I asked if her guests would pay her $8,000 to have the wedding on Saturday. She laughed. Then she got quiet. She moved her wedding to Sunday — not Friday, but Sunday — and saved $5,000. Her guest count dropped by eight people. She sent them handwritten notes apologizing. They all understood. One of them told her "I would rather miss your wedding than have you start your marriage in debt." That is a real friend.

Now, if you are considering a Friday wedding, here is a checklist I put together from my own experience and from watching couples who did it successfully.

Send save-the-dates early. Like, nine months early. People need time to request time off. Friday weddings require more notice than Saturdays. We sent ours ten months out. Almost everyone who wanted to come made it work.

Negotiate with vendors using your Friday date as leverage. When I called photographers, I led with "I am looking for a Friday in June." Several of them immediately offered lower rates without me even asking. They want to fill their weekdays. You have power here. Use it.

Build in buffer time for setup. You can not do everything Friday morning. You will be stressed. We rented the venue from Thursday noon to Friday midnight — 36 hours — for only $200 more than the 8-hour Friday rental. That gave us Thursday to decorate, run the sound check, and drop off supplies. Friday morning, I got my hair done, had coffee, and did not panic. Worth every penny.

Be prepared for a few no's. Some people will not come. That is okay. Your wedding is for you, not for the 85th cousin you have not seen in a decade. The people who matter will make it work.

Check your VIP list first. Before you book a Friday, call your parents, siblings, wedding party, and closest friends. Ask them directly: "Can you make a Friday work?" If your mom says she can not get time off, that is a problem. If your best man says he will take a half-day, fine. Know your non-negotiables.

One more thing about Friday weddings — the energy is different. It feels more intimate. People are not rushing from another wedding or a full day of errands. They have taken the day off. They are present. Our Friday wedding had a relaxed, almost mid-week dinner party vibe. Guests told us they loved not having to choose between three weddings on the same Saturday.

Sam and Alex did a Sunday wedding of 120 people and saved even more than we did. Sunday vendors are often cheaper than Friday because they are harder to fill. But Sunday means guests might leave early because of work Monday. Sam and Alex had a brunch wedding — 11 AM ceremony, lunch reception, done by 4 PM. Their venue was $3,000 cheaper than Saturday. They told me "we saved $10,000 and still had a blast."

So Friday is not the only alternative. Thursday, Sunday, Monday — all cheaper. But Friday was the sweet spot for us. Enough savings. Enough attendance. Enough recovery time before Monday.

I will leave you with this. The wedding industry will tell you that Saturday is the only "real" wedding day. That is marketing. The day after your wedding, no one cares what day it was. They remember how they felt. Ours felt like love, not a spreadsheet. But the spreadsheet helped us get there.

Maya sent me a photo of her honeymoon in Costa Rica last month. The caption read: "This is brought to you by $5,000 in Sunday savings." Get that money.
