Sustainable Weddings: Do They Actually Save Money?

By Olivia Bennett June 17, 2026 4 min read

Sustainable Weddings: Do They Actually Save Money?

Emma walked into my office with a canvas bag full of mason jars. She set them on my desk one by one. Twelve jars. Clean. No labels. She had already washed them.

"I want a zero-waste wedding," she said. "And I want to spend $15,000."

I told her those two goals might conflict. She said "try anyway."

We did. And we learned something surprising. Sustainable choices saved her $3,000.

Her venue was a public park with a historic lodge. No need for decor. The lodge had wood floors, big windows, and a stone fireplace. She added zero flowers. She used potted plants from a local nursery as centerpieces, then returned them after the wedding for a 50% refund.

Guests brought their own plates from home. This sounds crazy. It worked. People brought their favorite mug or bowl. The table looked eclectic and personal. No rental fees. No paper plates. No plastic.

Food came from a local farm-to-table caterer who used compostable containers. The cost was the same as a regular caterer. No savings, but no extra cost either.

Cash gifts instead of physical items. No packaging waste. No returns. And they used that cash to buy second-hand furniture for their new apartment.

The park lodge cost $1,500 for the day. A traditional venue with similar charm would have been $6,000. The park had no required vendors. No mandatory flower budget. No corkage fees. The savings were massive.

DIY flowers using local seasonal blooms: $200 vs $2,000 pro. DIY invitations using recycled paper and digital RSVPs: $50 vs $500. DIY favors — they made jam from berries they picked themselves: $40 vs $300. Total DIY savings: $2,510.

Not every sustainable choice saves money. Organic, fair-trade, carbon-neutral — those often cost more. But many sustainable choices are just old-fashioned frugality. Use what you have. Borrow instead of buying new. Rent instead of owning. These choices are good for the planet and your wallet.

Jade wanted a sustainable wedding. She also wanted a designer dress. The dress was shipped from overseas, wrapped in plastic, and would be worn once. That is not sustainable. She bought a vintage dress from a local consignment shop for $300. No shipping. No plastic. No new materials. She saved $2,700.

She moved the dress savings to her food budget. That gave her a late-night snack bar and a second dessert option. Guests were happy. The planet was slightly happier.

Sustainable weddings take more work. You have to research. You have to call around. You have to explain to your mom why there are no plastic straws. But the savings are real.

A few easy swaps:

Paperless invitations. Save $200-500. Save trees.

Potted centerpieces. Instead of cut flowers. Cheaper, reusable, compostable.

Second-hand decor. Facebook Marketplace. Craigslist. Buy used, sell after. Net cost close to zero.

Buffet instead of plated. Less food waste. Lower cost.

Local beer and wine instead of full bar. Less shipping waste. Lower alcohol budget.

Sam and Alex did a fully zero-waste wedding for $10k. They got married in a city park. They served a potluck meal. They asked guests to bring a dish instead of a gift. The food was amazing. The variety was incredible. No waste. No debt. They told me "it was the most fun wedding we have ever been to."

Not everyone should do potluck. But there is a spectrum. On one end: $80k traditional wedding with imported flowers and plastic everything. On the other: $5k potluck in a park with compostable plates. Most of us land somewhere in between. Every step toward sustainability is also a step toward lower costs.

Emma and Noah used the $3k they saved to adopt a rescue dog. The dog wore a little bow tie in their wedding photos. Zero waste. Maximum joy.
