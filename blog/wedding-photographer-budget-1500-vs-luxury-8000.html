Wedding Photographer: Budget ($1,500) vs. Luxury ($8,000)

By Olivia Bennett June 22, 2026 4 min read

Wedding Photographer: Budget ($1,500) vs. Luxury ($8,000)

$1,500 and $8,000. Same wedding. Same couple. Same venue. The difference is experience, deliverables, and marketing.

I have worked with both ends of the spectrum. A $1,500 photographer delivered photos that made me cry. An $8,000 photographer delivered over-edited, soulless images. Price does not guarantee quality. But it does guarantee something. Experience. Backup equipment. Insurance. A second shooter. A contract that protects you.

Under $2,000. Usually a newer photographer building their portfolio. They might have shot 5-10 weddings. They might not have backup equipment. They might not have insurance. They will likely work alone — no second shooter. Delivery time can be 4-8 weeks. Editing may be inconsistent. But some of these photographers are incredibly talented. They just have not raised their prices yet.

$2,000-4,000. The sweet spot for most couples. Experienced photographer. Backup equipment. Insurance. 6-8 hours of coverage. One shooter. Engagement session included. Delivery 4-6 weeks. High-quality editing. This is where most couples should look.

$4,000-6,000. Top-tier in most cities. 8-10 hours of coverage. Second shooter often included. Engagement session. Album credit. Delivery 3-4 weeks. Very experienced. This is for couples who prioritize photography above almost everything else.

$6,000+. Luxury photographers. Often national or international. 10+ hours. Two shooters. Engagement session. Album. Print rights. Fast delivery. These photographers shoot for magazines. They are artists. They are also marketing experts. You are paying for their brand as much as their photos.

Set the photography slider to 10% of your total budget. For a $30k wedding, that is $3,000. That is realistic. Do not spend 20% on photos unless photos are your top priority.

Rachel had a $20k budget. She wanted to spend $4k on photography. That is 20%. I asked her what she would cut. She said "I do not know." We moved the slider. At 20%, her venue budget dropped by $2k. She found a venue she liked less. She stuck with 10% photography. She was happy.

If you have 100 guests, a $4k photographer costs $40 per guest. That is more than your cake. Is it worth it to you? Only you can answer.

What to look for in a photographer, regardless of price:

Full gallery. Ask to see a complete wedding gallery, not just highlights. Anyone can show you their best 20 photos. The full gallery shows consistency.

Backup equipment. What happens if their camera breaks during your first dance? A pro has a second camera. A budget photographer might not.

Contract. Read it. What is the delivery timeline? What is the cancellation policy? What is the backup plan if they are sick?

Editing style. Look for natural skin tones, not over-processed. Avoid trends that will look dated in five years.

Second shooter. Do you need one? For 100+ guests, yes. One photographer can not be in two places at once.

Mike hired a $1,800 photographer. The photographer showed up late, left early, and delivered grainy photos. Mike was devastated. He told me "I thought I was saving money." He was not. He lost $1,800 and his wedding photos. He ended up asking guests for their cell phone pictures.

That is the risk at the low end. Some $1,500 photographers are amazing. Some are terrible. You have to vet them carefully.

If you want a $3k photographer, save $115 a week for six months. That is doable.

My honest advice: do not skimp on photography. These are the only tangible thing you keep from your wedding. The flowers die. The food gets eaten. The dress goes in a box. The photos stay on your wall.

But do not overspend either. A $6k photographer will not make you happier than a $3k one. The difference is often marketing, not talent.

Mike eventually got a friend to re-edit his grainy photos. They look okay now. But he still regrets not spending an extra $1k for a pro.
