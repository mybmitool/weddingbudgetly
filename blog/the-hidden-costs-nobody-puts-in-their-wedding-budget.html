The Hidden Costs Nobody Puts in Their Wedding Budget

By Olivia Bennett June 18, 2026 5 min read

The Hidden Costs Nobody Puts in Their Wedding Budget

Ryan signed a venue contract for $8,000. Then they hit him with the $1,200 "service charge." Then the $500 "delivery fee." Then the $300 "cake cutting fee." Then the $400 "overtime charge" because his guests stayed 45 minutes past the contracted end time.

His $8,000 venue just became $10,400. A 30% surprise.

I have seen this happen so many times. Couples budget carefully. They track every line item. Then they forget about the fees that are not listed on the pretty brochure. Those fees can add 20-40% to your final bill.

Service charge. This is the big one. Many venues and caterers add 18-22% to your total bill. They call it a "service charge" or "administrative fee." Ask what it covers. Sometimes it includes gratuity. Sometimes it does not. If it does not, you will need to tip on top of it — another 15-20%. A $10,000 catering bill with a 20% service charge ($2,000) plus 20% gratuity ($2,000) becomes $14,000. That is $4,000 in fees. Ask upfront: "Is gratuity included in the service charge?" Get the answer in writing.

Delivery and setup fees. Florists, rental companies, and bakeries charge to bring their items to your venue. I have seen delivery fees as low as $50 and as high as $500. Some vendors include delivery in their quote. Most do not. Ask. Then add it to your budget.

Overtime charges. Your venue rental is 6 PM to midnight. At 12:01 AM, the clock starts ticking. Overtime can be $500-1,000 per hour for the venue, plus $200-300 per hour for each vendor — DJ, photographer, bartenders. One extra hour can cost $2,000. Build a buffer in your timeline. End the party 30 minutes before your contracted time to avoid the rush.

Cake cutting fee. This one infuriates me. You bring a cake. The venue charges $2-5 per slice to cut and serve it. For 150 guests, that is $300-750. For what? Cutting a cake takes five minutes. Ask if this fee can be waived. Sometimes it can. Sometimes you can have a family member do the cutting. Sometimes you are stuck. Either way, know about it in advance.

Corkage fee. If you bring your own alcohol, some venues charge $10-20 per bottle to open and serve it. That is $200-400 for a moderate bar. Ask if you can pour your own wine. Some venues allow it. Some do not.

Gratuity. Vendors expect tips. Photographer $200-500, DJ $100-300, hair and makeup $50-150 each, officiant $100-200, band $50-100 per member. For a full-service wedding, gratuities can total $2,000-3,000. Budget for this separately. Do not forget it.

Tax. Sales tax or lodging tax adds 5-15% depending on your location. On a $30k wedding, that is $1,500-4,500. Tax is not negotiable. Include it in your budget from the start.

Marriage license fee. $30-150 depending on your county. This is small, but couples forget it.

Hotel room blocks. If you block hotel rooms for guests, you might be responsible for any unused rooms if you do not meet your block minimum. Read the contract carefully.

Ryan and Jess almost signed a venue contract with $4,000 in undisclosed fees. I asked the coordinator for a "full estimate including all taxes, service charges, and gratuities." She emailed it. The original quote was $12,000. The full estimate was $16,200. Ryan almost fainted. They walked away. They found a venue that was transparent about pricing. They paid $14,000 total and slept better.

If you know you have $5,000 in hidden fees, add that to your target. Save for it. Do not let it surprise you.

Your checklist for avoiding hidden fees:

Ask every vendor for an "all-in" quote. Say these exact words: "Please provide a total out-the-door price including all taxes, service charges, delivery fees, and gratuities." If they refuse, walk away.

Read the contract twice. The hidden fees are always in the fine print. Look for "service charge," "administration fee," "setup fee," "breakdown fee," "overtime rate," "cleaning fee," "cake cutting fee," "corkage fee."

Ask about overtime rates before you sign. What is the hourly rate after 11 PM? After midnight? Write it down.

Build a 15-20% contingency line item in your budget calculator. Call it "unexpected fees." You will use some of it. Maybe all of it.

Pay attention to minimums. Some vendors have a minimum spend that you might not meet. If you do not meet it, they add the difference as a fee. Ask: "Is there a minimum? What happens if we do not meet it?"

Taylor got a $600 "bar minimum" fee because her guests did not drink enough. She did not know there was a minimum. She thought "consumption bar" meant pay for what people drink. It did. But there was also a $600 minimum. Her guests drank $400 worth. She paid $200 extra. She did not read the fine print.

Ryan and Jess — the ones who walked away from the $16k venue — they ended up getting married at a public garden for $4k all-in. No hidden fees. They used the $12k they saved as a down payment on a house. The house has a garden. They have anniversaries there now.
