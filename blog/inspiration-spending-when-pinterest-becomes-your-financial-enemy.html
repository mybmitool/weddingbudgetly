Inspiration Spending: When Pinterest Becomes Your Financial Enemy

By Olivia Bennett June 16, 2026 4 min read

Inspiration Spending: When Pinterest Becomes Your Financial Enemy

Dana's Pinterest board had 400 pins. Four hundred. I asked her to pick her top five. She could not. She was in love with everything. Her budget was $25k. Her inspiration board would cost $80k.

This is inspiration spending. You see something beautiful online. You convince yourself you need it. Then you spend money you did not plan to spend on something you did not know existed six months ago.

Start with your total. Then add a new line item for every Pinterest trend. Flower wall? Add $1,000. Champagne tower? Add $500. Live painter? Add $1,500. Late night snack bar? Add $800. The calculator will turn red. That red is your friend. It is telling you "you can not afford all of this. Choose."

The average bride saves 200-500 images during wedding planning. That is not inspiration. That is anxiety. The more you save, the more you feel you need. The more you need, the more you spend.

Dana deleted Pinterest. She planned her wedding from a single magazine. She ended up under budget.

That live painter is $25 per guest. That champagne tower is $8 per guest. That flower wall is $17 per guest. Add them up, and you have added $50 per person to your wedding. For 100 guests, that is $5,000. For what? Photos for social media.

How to break the cycle.

Delete Pinterest for two weeks. Just two weeks. See if you miss it. Most people do not. They are relieved.

Create a "must have" list of five things. Not fifty. Five. The venue. The food. The photographer. The dress. The music. Everything else is optional. If it is not on your five, you do not need it.

Run every potential purchase through the DIY Savings Calculator.

✂️
DIY Savings Calculator

That $800 balloon arch? DIY cost $200. Time 4 hours. Savings $600, hourly rate $150. Worth it? Maybe. That $2,000 custom neon sign? DIY impossible. Pro only. Question: do you really need a neon sign?

Unfollow wedding accounts that make you feel poor. If a certain influencer's wedding makes you jealous, unfollow. That feeling is not inspiration. It is inadequacy. You do not need to see other people's budgets.

A friend of mine spent $3,000 on a floral installation that she saw on Instagram. The installation was a giant ring of flowers hanging above the dance floor. It was beautiful. No one looked at it. The photos showed it. But they were the only ones who ever looked at those photos. She told me "I wish I had spent that $3,000 on better food."

That is the inspiration spending trap. You spend money on things that only look good from one angle, in one photo, on one day. Your guests do not notice. Your future self will not care.

Every time you resist a Pinterest trend, add that money to your savings tracker. Watch the number go up. That is more satisfying than any flower wall.

The wedding industry has a term for couples like us: "the comparison economy." We see what others have. We want the same. We spend to keep up. The industry profits. We go into debt.

Break the loop.

Dana used the $5,000 she saved to take her whole family out for a nice dinner the night before the wedding. She told me "that dinner was worth more than any flower wall."
