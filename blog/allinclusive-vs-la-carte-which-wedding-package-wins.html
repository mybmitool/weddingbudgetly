All-Inclusive vs. À La Carte: Which Wedding Package Wins?

By Olivia Bennett June 20, 2026 4 min read

All-Inclusive vs. À La Carte: Which Wedding Package Wins?

Lisa sat across from me with two brochures. One said "all-inclusive wedding package: $28,000." The other said "venue rental: $15,000. Vendors not included." She had been planning for six months. She was exhausted.

"I just want someone else to handle it," she said. She almost signed the all-inclusive.

I asked her to list what the all-inclusive actually covered. She pulled up the brochure. Venue, catering, bar, basic flowers, DJ, coordinator, cake, and a set menu. I asked what was not included. She read the fine print. "Photography not included. Attire not included. Honeymoon not included. Tips not included. Overtime not included. Upgrades not included." I asked her to estimate those missing items. She added $8,000. Her $28k package became $36k.

The à la carte option looked like this: venue $15k, catering $7k, bar $2k, flowers $1k, DJ $1k, coordinator $1k, cake $500. Total $27,500. Plus photography $3k, attire $2k, tips $2k. Total $34,500. The à la carte was actually cheaper. And she could choose her own vendors.

She chose à la carte. She told me "I almost paid $1,500 more for less control."

She built two budgets. The all-inclusive looked simpler on the surface. The à la carte looked messier. But the numbers did not lie. À la carte was cheaper and more flexible.

When does all-inclusive make sense?

You have no time. You are planning from another city. You have demanding jobs. You do not want to interview five photographers and three florists. One contract, one payment, one point of contact. That convenience costs money, but it saves sanity.

You have a small guest list (under 50). All-inclusive packages for intimate weddings can be reasonable. I have seen micro-wedding packages for $8-12k that include everything but the dress and rings. For a small group, the per-person cost can be competitive.

You are not picky. If you do not care which photographer, which flowers, which cake flavor, all-inclusive is fine. If you have strong opinions, you will end up paying for upgrades. And upgrades at all-inclusive venues are rarely cheap.

For a 100-person wedding, all-inclusive often runs $200-300 per person. À la carte averages $150-250. The difference is $5,000-10,000. That is a honeymoon.

The hidden downside of all-inclusive: the vendors are often the venue's in-house team or preferred list. They have no incentive to give you a great price. They know you are locked in. I have seen in-house DJs charge $2k for what a freelance DJ would charge $1k. I have seen in-house florists charge $3k for basic centerpieces. You can not shop around. You pay what they ask.

Tessa was locked into an all-inclusive venue. She wanted a specific photographer. The venue said "you have to use our preferred list." Her only options were photographers she did not love. She felt stuck. She ended up paying $500 extra to "buy out" the preferred vendor clause so she could bring her own. That $500 was pure waste.

My honest advice: get quotes for both. Build a spreadsheet. Include every single line item. Then ask yourself how much you would pay for convenience. For some couples, $5k is worth not interviewing vendors. For most, it is not.

If you choose à la carte and save $5k, that is 25 weeks of saving $200 a week. Watch that bar fill up. That is motivation.

I am not against all-inclusive. I have recommended it to couples who were overwhelmed and had the budget. But I have also seen couples regret it because they felt trapped or overpaid. Go in with your eyes open.

Lisa and Matt — the à la carte couple — they ended up $1,500 under their original estimate. They used that money to add a photo booth. They told me "we are so glad we did not take the easy way out."
