The Wedding I Planned For 15000 That Cost 28000 And Why I Do Not Regret It

2026-07-15 · 5 min read · General

The Wedding I Planned for $15,000 That Cost $28,000 and Why I Do Not Regret It

I sat at the kitchen table in our apartment in Portland, staring at a spreadsheet that said $27,847, and I laughed. Not a happy laugh. A hysterical laugh. The kind of laugh that comes out when your brain cannot process the gap between what you planned and what actually happened. We had budgeted $15,000 for our wedding. We had spent $27,847. We had gone 86% over budget. And the wedding was in three weeks.

I am a wedding planner. I have planned over 200 weddings. I have managed budgets from $5,000 to $500,000. I have negotiated with florists, caterers, photographers, and venues. I have handled last-minute crises, family drama, and weather disasters. And I had just blown my own wedding budget by $12,847 because I fell into every trap I warn my clients about.

It started with the venue. We wanted an outdoor wedding in the Columbia River Gorge. We found a vineyard — beautiful, scenic, perfect. The base price was $6,000. That was 40% of our budget. But it included nothing. No chairs. No tables. No linens. No catering. No alcohol. Just the view. And the view was expensive.

We rented chairs. $800. We rented tables. $600. We rented linens. $400. We hired a caterer. $4,200. We bought alcohol. $1,800. We hired a bartender. $500. The venue that cost $6,000 now cost $14,300. And we had not even gotten to the dress, the flowers, the photographer, or the music.

The dress was supposed to be $1,500. I found one I loved for $2,800. It was over budget. It was perfect. I bought it. The alterations were $450. The veil was $200. The shoes were $180. The jewelry was $120. The dress that was supposed to cost $1,500 cost $3,750. And I did not care. Because I was the bride. And the bride is allowed to go over budget. That is what I told myself. That is what every bride tells herself. And that is why weddings go over budget.

The flowers were supposed to be $800. The florist quoted $1,400. She said, "For a wedding this size, with this venue, $800 will look sparse." I said, "Do what you need to do." She did. The flowers were beautiful. They were also $1,400. The photographer was supposed to be $2,000. The one we wanted was $3,200. He was booked. We found another for $2,800. Still over budget. Still necessary. Because you cannot have a wedding without photos. You just cannot.

The guest list was supposed to be 80 people. It grew to 112. My mother added cousins I had never met. My mother-in-law added coworkers from her book club. My fiancé, Jake, added friends from college he had not spoken to in five years. Each guest cost $85 in food and alcohol. Thirty-two extra guests cost $2,720. And we needed a bigger tent. $800. And more tables and chairs. $400. And more linens. $200. The guest list that grew by 32 people cost an extra $4,120.

By the time we added the DJ ($1,200), the cake ($650), the invitations ($380), the favors ($240), the rehearsal dinner ($1,800), and the honeymoon deposit ($2,000), we were at $27,847. We had $153 left for emergencies. And we had three weeks to go.

I cried. I cried at the kitchen table. Jake held me. He said, "We can cut things." I said, "What? The flowers? The photographer? The food?" He said, "We can elope." I said, "I do not want to elope. I want this wedding. I just did not want it to cost this much." He said, "Then we will figure it out."

We figured it out. We took $5,000 from our honeymoon fund. We took $4,000 from our emergency savings. We put $3,847 on a credit card at 14% APR. We paid for the wedding. And we got married on a Saturday in September, in the Columbia River Gorge, with 112 people, beautiful flowers, a perfect dress, and a photographer who captured every moment.

And it was worth it. Not because it was perfect. It was not. The DJ played the wrong song for our first dance. The cake was slightly lopsided. My mother's cousin got drunk and told everyone about my childhood bedwetting. But it was our wedding. And it was beautiful. And it was real. And it was worth $27,847.

I tell my clients this story now. I tell them about the $15,000 budget. I tell them about the $27,847 reality. I tell them about the dress, the flowers, the guest list. And I tell them that weddings go over budget because they matter. Because you cannot put a price on memory. Because you cannot negotiate with emotion. Because you are not buying a product. You are buying an experience. And experiences are expensive.

But I also tell them to add 30% to their budget. Because things will go wrong. Because you will find a dress you love that costs more than you planned. Because your guest list will grow. Because your mother will add cousins. Because the florist will convince you that $800 is not enough. Because weddings are not spreadsheets. They are stories. And stories have unexpected chapters.

We paid off the credit card in eight months. We took a smaller honeymoon — a weekend in Bend instead of two weeks in Italy. We rebuilt our savings. And we have photos. We have memories. We have a story. And every time I look at our wedding album, I remember the spreadsheet that said $27,847. And I smile. Because some things are worth going over budget for.

— Olivia, from Portland, where the weddings are beautiful and the budgets are finally honest.
