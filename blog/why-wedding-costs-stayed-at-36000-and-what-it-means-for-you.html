Why Wedding Costs Stayed at $36,000 (And What It Means for You)

By Olivia Bennett June 14, 2026 6 min read

Why Wedding Costs Stayed at $36,000 (And What It Means for You)

Nina sat across from me at a coffee shop on Hawthorne, her laptop open to a spreadsheet. She had been planning for six months. Her budget was $28,000. She had already booked the venue ($9,000) and the photographer ($4,500). She was looking at caterers. The quotes were coming in at $12,000-15,000. She looked at the numbers, then at me, then back at the numbers. Her pen tapped the table three times.

"This is impossible," she said. "I can not do this without going over."

She was not wrong. But she was not entirely right either.

The average wedding in America costs $36,000. That number has not moved in two years.

After a decade of steady increases — $28k in 2015, $32k in 2019, $35k in 2024 — the number flatlined. Some industry reports call it a plateau. I call it a reality check. Couples are finally saying no to the upcharge.

I have watched this shift from inside the industry. Five years ago, no one questioned the $10k venue fee or the $5k flower bill. Now, couples come to me with spreadsheets and questions. "Why does this cost so much?" "Can I do it myself?" "What if we just skip it?"

These questions are healthy. They are also why the average cost stopped climbing. Couples are choosing smaller guest lists, non-Saturday dates, and DIY options. They are rejecting the "you only get married once" justification for overspending.

Let me show you where that $36k actually goes, based on 2026 data from real weddings.

Venue: $10,800. That is down from $12k in 2024. Couples are choosing non-traditional venues — parks, community centers, private estates rented hourly — instead of "wedding barns" with $10k minimums.

Catering: $9,000. Food costs are up. But couples are switching to buffets, food trucks, and drop catering. The per-person average held steady because of these choices, not despite them.

Photography: $3,600. This is flat. Photographers have not raised rates much because there is more competition than ever. Every art school grad with a camera wants to shoot weddings.

Flowers: $2,880. This category is down 15% from 2024. DIY is eating the florist's lunch. Couples are buying from farms, using silk flowers, or skipping elaborate installations.

Attire: $2,160. Steady. The wedding dress industry is struggling. Millennial brides bought $3k dresses. Gen Z brides rent, buy secondhand, or wear something they already own.

Music: $1,800. DJs are cheaper than bands. Spotify playlists are cheaper than DJs. This number will keep dropping.

Everything else: $5,760. Rentals, stationery, transportation, gifts, misc. This category is the easiest to cut.

Every ten guests cost about $1,500-2,000. If you cut 20 guests, you save $3-4k. That is real money.

So why did costs stop rising? Three reasons.

Gen Z is budget-conscious. This generation grew up watching millennial siblings drown in student debt and wedding loans. They are not repeating the mistake. A survey from 2026 found that 43% of Gen Z couples set a budget under $20k. That is forcing vendors to compete on price, not prestige.

The economy is uncertain. Inflation is high. Interest rates are high. Job security is questionable. Couples are keeping cash on hand instead of throwing it at a party. The "go big because YOLO" mindset is gone.

Information is power. Ten years ago, you called a venue and trusted their quote. Now you can check Reddit, read Google reviews, and see real wedding recaps on Instagram. You know what things should cost. Vendors can not hide behind opaque pricing anymore.

Nina used this information to negotiate $4k off her venue. She found another couple's wedding recap online. The recap included the exact price they paid — $2k less than what the venue quoted Nina. She sent the venue a screenshot. They matched the price. She told me "I would have paid their first quote if I had not seen that post."

That is the new landscape. Transparency is driving prices down.

What does the flat average mean for you? It means you do not have to spend $36k. That is an average. Half of couples spend less. Many spend much less. The average is pulled up by the 10% of weddings that cost $80k or more. Ignore them. They are not you.

Priya had a $20k wedding that everyone thought cost $50k. Her secret? She spent money on the things people see and feel — venue, food, music — and saved on things people do not notice — flowers, favors, paper goods. She used the budget calculator to shift money from one category to another. The result looked expensive. The actual cost was not.

The average couple spends $10k on venue. The average couple spends $3k on a backyard setup — tent, tables, chairs, portable bathrooms, generator. That is a $7k difference. That is a used car. That is a semester of college. That is a year of rent. Ask yourself: is the "venue experience" worth $7k to you? For some, yes. For many, no.

I am not saying skip the venue. I am saying know what you are buying.

The wedding industry wants you to believe that $36k is normal. It is not. It is an average. Averages include everyone — the $5k courthouse wedding and the $150k mansion wedding. You get to choose where you land.

Nina — who negotiated her venue down by $4k — used that money to extend her honeymoon from five days to nine. She told me "the venue looked exactly the same. The extra four days in Italy were unforgettable."
