Honeymoon Budget Planning: From Bali to Banff Without Bankruptcy

Honeymoon Budget Planning: From Bali to Banff Without Bankruptcy

Katie sat across from me with two brochures in her lap. One showed an overwater bungalow in Bora Bora. The other showed a cabin in Banff. She looked at the Bora Bora brochure for ten seconds, then at the Banff one for two. Then she sighed and put both down.

"We have $4,000 left after the wedding," she said. "Where do we go?"

I have seen couples blow their entire wedding budget surplus on a honeymoon they could not afford. I have seen couples skip the honeymoon altogether because they had nothing left. Neither is ideal.

In 2026, the average honeymoon costs about $5,500. But that is an average. A week in Bali runs $3,000-6,000. A week in Banff runs $2,500-4,000. A week in Paris runs $5,000-8,000. The difference is thousands of dollars. And most of that difference comes down to choices, not luck.

Katie and Ryan ended up in Puerto Vallarta, Mexico. All-inclusive resort, five nights, flights and hotel for both: $2,800. They went there. They loved it. They came back with money left over.

Choosing a destination that fit their budget — that was the difference.

Let me be clear: I do not recommend borrowing for a honeymoon. But some couples do. And if you are going to do it, know the cost. A $5,000 honeymoon loan at 12% for 24 months costs $5,636 total. That is $636 in interest. Is a week in Mexico worth $636 in interest? Maybe. But at least you are making an informed choice.

Smarter: set up a honeymoon fund on your wedding registry. Many platforms allow guests to contribute cash toward specific experiences — "dinner on the beach" or "sunset sail" or "upgrade to the suite." A couple I know got $2,500 in honeymoon contributions. That paid for their flights. They covered the rest with savings. No debt.

Anna and her husband opened a separate savings account labeled "Honeymoon" and automatically transferred $50 a week from their paychecks for two years. That is $5,200. They did not miss the money because they never saw it. When the wedding was over, they had a fully funded trip waiting.

Here is my honest advice on honeymoon budgeting.

Do not go right away. I know the tradition is to leave the next morning. But you will be exhausted. You will be emotionally drained. You will not enjoy the trip as much. Wait a month. Take a long weekend to decompress. Then go. You will save money on last-minute flights, too.

Consider traveling during off-season. The same resort in Cancun is $500 a night in February and $200 in September. September is hurricane season — but the risk is low. Talk to a travel agent. They will tell you the real story.

Use points if you have them. Many couples pay for wedding expenses with a travel rewards card, then use the points for the honeymoon. I know a couple who put $25k of wedding costs on a card that earned 2% back. That is $500 in points — enough for a round-trip flight for one person. Just make sure you pay off the card immediately.

Skip the all-inclusive unless you drink a lot. All-inclusives charge for everything up front. If you do not drink alcohol, you are subsidizing people who do. A regular hotel plus paying for meals as you go is often cheaper.

Look for package deals. Costco Travel. CheapCaribbean. Vacation express. Some packages bundle flights, hotel, and transfers for less than booking separately.

Dave took his honeymoon to Banff. They rented a camper van for $100 a night, cooked their own meals, and hiked every day. Total cost for 10 days: $2,500. He told me "it was the best trip of my life. And I did not have to share a wall with anyone."

Not just for weddings: the Per-Guest Cost Estimator. You can adapt the logic to your honeymoon. Cost per day instead of cost per person. Set a daily budget. Stick to it.

After the chaos, the honeymoon is for you two to connect. It does not need to be Instagram-perfect. It does not need to be Bali or Paris or the Maldives. It needs to be a place where you can breathe.

I still think about my Costa Rica honeymoon. The outdoor shower. The howler monkeys waking us up at 5 AM. The simple breakfast of fresh fruit and coffee. None of it was expensive. All of it was perfect.

Katie and Ryan — the couple who went to Mexico — they still talk about the street tacos they ate from a cart. Not the resort restaurant. The street cart. Total cost for dinner that night: $6. They still laugh about it.
