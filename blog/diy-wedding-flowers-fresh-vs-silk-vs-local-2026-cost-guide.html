DIY Wedding Flowers: Fresh vs. Silk vs. Local (2026 Cost Guide)

By Olivia Bennett June 08, 2026 5 min read

DIY Wedding Flowers: Fresh vs. Silk vs. Local (2026 Cost Guide)

Walking into a florist's studio in March, I watched a bride's face fall. The quote was $3,800. Her budget for flowers was $800. She looked at the arrangement of peonies and garden roses on the counter, then at her phone, then back at the flowers. Her shoulders dropped half an inch. She did not say anything for ten seconds. Then she thanked the florist and walked out.

That bride called me the next day. She had $800. She wanted flowers. What were her options?

Three options, actually. Fresh local. Fresh imported. And silk. Each has a price, a look, and a hidden cost that nobody talks about.

Fresh local flowers come from farms within 100 miles of your venue. In Portland, that means Sauvie Island, the Willamette Valley, and a handful of urban farms inside the city limits. The price in 2026: $25-40 per bucket, depending on the season. A bucket holds enough stems for 2-3 centerpieces or one bridal bouquet. For a wedding with 15 tables, you need about 8 buckets. That is $200-320 in raw materials. Add $50 for ribbon, tape, and floral scissors. Total: $250-370.

The catch? You have to arrange them yourself. Or find a friend who will. The morning of my wedding, I woke up at 5 AM, drove to the farm, loaded dahlias and zinnias into my trunk, and spent three hours in my garage with my mother-in-law, trimming stems and jamming them into mason jars. My hands smelled like chlorophyll for two days. The ferns I bought from Home Depot for $12 each became centerpieces, then houseplants, then gifts for guests who admired them.

Fresh imported flowers — roses from Ecuador, peonies from Chile, ranunculus from Italy — arrive at the Portland Flower Market on Tuesdays. They are stunning. They are also $3-8 per stem. A bridal bouquet needs 25-35 stems. That is $75-280 for one bouquet. Centerpieces run $40-120 each. For 15 tables plus bouquets, boutonnieres, and ceremony decor, you are looking at $2,000-4,000 before labor. The florist's quote of $3,800 was not inflated. It was market rate.

Silk flowers in 2026 are not what they were ten years ago. High-end silk peonies, ranunculus, and anemones cost $4-12 per stem. They look real in photos. They do not wilt. They do not need water. And after the wedding, you can sell them on Facebook Marketplace for 40-60% of what you paid. A bride named Clara bought $600 worth of silk flowers for her June wedding, arranged them herself over two weekends, and sold the lot for $340 two weeks after the wedding. Net cost: $260. Her fresh-flower quote had been $2,200.

I ran the numbers for Clara. Professional florist: $2,200. DIY silk: $600 materials + 12 hours labor. She sold the silk after for $340. Net cost: $260. Savings: $1,940. Her hourly rate: $161. Worth it. But then I ran the numbers for a bride who wanted fresh local DIY. Materials: $320. Labor: 10 hours. No resale value. Savings vs. pro: $2,000. Hourly rate: $200. Also worth it. The only losing option was imported fresh DIY. Materials: $1,800. Labor: 10 hours. No resale. Pro quote: $2,200. Savings: $400. Hourly rate: $40. Not worth the stress.

She moved the $1,940 flower savings into her photography budget. That got her a second shooter and an album. She told me later that the album mattered more than the flowers ever would have.

Here is what nobody tells you about DIY flowers. The week before the wedding, your garage becomes a flower shop. Coolers full of buckets. Stems everywhere. Your partner will step on a thorn and curse. Your mother will ask why the roses look "different from the picture." You will wake up at 4 AM the day of the wedding because you dreamed the dahlias wilted. They did not. But you will check anyway.

Silk flowers eliminate the wilting problem. They also eliminate the scent, the texture, and the slight imperfection that makes fresh flowers feel alive. In photos, silk wins. In person, fresh wins. If your priority is Instagram, go silk. If your priority is memory, go fresh.

Local flower farms in the Portland area: Sauvie Island Farms (open Saturdays, cash only), Portland Flower Market (wholesale, open to public Tuesdays), and dozens of u-pick farms in the Willamette Valley. Call two weeks ahead. Ask what will be in season. Do not ask for peonies in October. The farmer will laugh at you.

One last thing. The bride who walked out of the florist's studio with the $3,800 quote? She ended up with a mix. Local fresh for the bouquets and ceremony. Silk for the centerpieces. Total cost: $580. She texted me a photo of her bouquet the morning of the wedding. The caption was just a smiley face. No words needed.
